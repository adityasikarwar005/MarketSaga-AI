Most of what a research system finds is nothing.

MarketSage is an event-driven options research system for Indian index derivatives. It forecasts a distribution, prices structures against a fitted volatility surface, sizes on the pessimistic end of its own interval, and refuses the trade when the edge does not survive costs. Five of its models have been measured on real market data. All five lost. That result is on this page because a system that only displays its successes cannot be used to decide anything.

What has actually been measured

Every result here is a loss, and each one is recorded with its cause.

The rest of the system runs on synthetic inputs whose answers were known in advance. That establishes the arithmetic and says nothing about edge. These five ran on real market data.

M35 — overnight gap

CRPS 2.4% worse than a naive baseline over 2,465 sessions, where gate G4 needs 10% better. The regularisation sweep improves to a plateau where the feature weights are essentially zero — the data saying the daily cross-asset features carry almost nothing.

M55 — volatility

+0.0% against HAR-RV on ten years of real bars. The residual booster’s own acceptance test declined to keep it in three folds of four: the model is reporting that it has nothing to add, not failing to fit.

M53 — direction

Log-loss delta +0.004 with a bootstrap interval straddling zero. Its own discrimination gate refused in four folds of four. The rules baseline scores 0.69289 where a coin flip is 0.69315 — neither has anything to say.

M54 — quantile heads

Beaten by their own Gaussian fallback on pinball loss, −2.2% at one day. Widening them until coverage reached nominal would tune the number the check exists to catch, so the consequence was measured instead: position sizing was running a third too large in the busy regime.

M63 — dealer gamma positioning

Built because every earlier model fed price history in and asked about price. Open interest by strike is not in the price series. Result: Spearman +0.117 against the HAR residual, permutation p = 0.189 on 128 sessions. Not a finding.

What beat it: the calendar

Days-to-expiry alone scores −0.243 against the same target — stronger than the entire options-based feature, with no chain data at all. Logged as a hypothesis, not a finding: it was noticed while looking at something else, on a block already examined.

Two gates are failing, and they are on the front page.

A gate is a threshold agreed before the measurement. Tuning until one passes is the anti-pattern the whole measurement layer exists to prevent, so results are recorded whether or not they pass.

How it is built

The machinery that makes a negative result trustworthy.

Any system can produce a number. These are the parts that decide whether the number means anything.

Null tests before results

Ordinary tests ask whether a model works when there is a signal. Every fitted component here also has to answer what it reports when there is nothing to find. The pattern across two full audits: each defect produced a working pipeline, plausible numbers, and no error.

Point-in-time, or it does not exist

Every record carries the time it happened, the time it was ingested and the time it became available. The replay engine releases nothing early, and asking the simulator for a price from before its own decision raises rather than answers.

A hash-chained experiment log

Every model evaluation is appended to a chained file, so deleting a disappointing run breaks the chain at a reportable index. A deflated Sharpe computed as if there had been one attempt after there were thirty is a decoration, not a correction.

Decisions leave a record

Changing a risk limit requires a written decision record naming what it makes worse and what would show it was a mistake. A limit that can be edited in a commit is one that gets edited after a losing week, by the person who lost it.

Read this before reading any number on the desk.

Every figure in the workspace came out of the real modules — the surface fitter, the path sampler, the quantile heads, the Greeks engine, the structure enumerator — run on synthetic inputs whose answers were known in advance. The fitted parameters return to six decimals and the calibrated paths land on the quantile heads to five, which means the code computes what it claims to compute.

It does not mean the system makes money. No order has ever reached a broker, and the mode indicator on the desk is structural rather than a badge for exactly that reason.
